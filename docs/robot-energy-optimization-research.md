# Energy-aware routing for mixed robot fleets

**Research brief · 19 November 2026**

For warehouse operators, robotics engineers, and OpenShift AI platform teams.

## What to pilot first

Give the dispatcher a watt-hour (Wh) estimate for each feasible **robot, route, and job** combination. A route can win because it avoids wheel slip, a tracked pivot, or a long loaded carry. A robot can win because it already sits near pickup, carries the pallet within its rating, or can finish and reach an available charger. Include the job from pickup through putaway and the necessary repositioning. Otherwise a policy can appear to save energy by moving the cost to the next job.

**Start with one instrumented wheeled robot and two approved corridors between the same endpoints.** Choose a real trade-off such as a short rough or stop-heavy corridor and a longer smooth one. Drive both under matched loads, measure pack Wh, and compare the current dispatcher, calibrated physics, and a learned correction. This is the shortest step from this repository to a customer result. Add a tracked vehicle to test whether the route ranking transfers. Add a forklift once pallet weight, mast state, and battery power can be joined to the same job.

There is a reason to test this physically. In two University of Minnesota field comparisons, a ground robot used **19.7% and 11.9% less drive energy** on longer routes. Its sensors and computer had a separate supply. In this repository's 121-mission simulation, all three policies chose the same corridor. The served model reduced mission-energy mean absolute error from **0.035405 to 0.001138 Wh**, while route energy was statistically indistinguishable. The former shows how a surface can change a decision. The latter gives customers a working forecasting baseline and a clear test for incremental value. [Wei & Isler, field experiments, §5.2.2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/wei20a/wei20a.pdf) · [Repository Stage 4 results](validation/warehouse-energy/phase-4/phase-4-report.md)

## How robot and load change the decision

| Machine | What the planner needs before dispatch | What to measure during commissioning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wheeled AMR | Drive and steering type, tare and carried mass, wheel/tire configuration, grade and surface by direction, speed profile, stops, turns and expected waits | Repeated straight runs, turns, ramps in both directions, stops and powered idle. Separate compute/sensor draw where separately supplied. A slower trip can cost more when auxiliaries run throughout. [Measured wheeled field study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/wei20a/wei20a.pdf) |
| Tracked skid-steer | Track configuration, surface condition, turn radius or heading change, straight distance, grade and payload. A tight pivot can consume energy with nearly zero displacement. | Compare a straight, a broad arc, and a pivot on each approved surface. Compare track speed with independent body motion to estimate slip. Hard-floor turning and travel over yielding ground exercise different losses. Learn a vehicle-by-surface interaction and allow either robot to win. [Penn State field tests](https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.22076) · [Tracked/wheeled deformable-terrain experiments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094114X18312783) |
| Electric forklift or autonomous lift truck | Truck and attachment, pallet mass and load centre, pickup/drop heights, empty approach and return, loaded travel, lift/lower mode, and permitted speed under load | Record load changes at pickup/drop, fork height and motion, hydraulic or lift-motor signal, traction and pack power. Test empty/light/heavy pallets and low/high racks on the same route. Check the truck's capacity chart for each height and load centre before comparing candidates. [Forklift duty-cycle study](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/18/9854) · [Toyota residual-capacity guide](https://toyota-forklifts.eu/guides/residual-capacity-guide/) |

**A forklift example.** Raising a 1,000 kg pallet by 3 m requires 29.4 kJ, or **8.2 Wh**, of mechanical work on the pallet alone. At an illustrative 60% pack-to-pallet efficiency that component would draw **13.6 Wh**. The carriage, pump, travel and dwell add costs. For a job with fixed pickup and rack height, every valid route must deliver the same lift. The dispatch levers are the capable truck, loaded distance, stops, queue time, and any permitted job sequencing. Credit lowering or braking only to the extent that the pack accepts measured return power.

**When a longer path stops paying.** The first Minnesota field comparison saved 918.6 J at the drive supply. If a customer robot drew an additional constant 30 W from its main pack for sensors and computing, roughly **31 extra seconds** on that path would consume the entire drive-energy advantage (918.6 J ÷ 30 W). This is a break-even calculation with an illustrative auxiliary load. Measuring both trip duration and whole-robot power tells the customer which route actually wins. [Published drive-energy measurements and separate sensor supply](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/wei20a/wei20a.pdf)

**A model the dispatcher can use.** Start with calibrated physics for rolling and grade resistance, acceleration, steering losses, auxiliary power over time, and mast work. Fit a small regression to the remaining error using metered jobs. Score directed segments and transitions with the expected load at that point. Represent a wait, pivot or lift as an action with duration and Wh even when its map length is zero. Fit interactions such as drive type × surface × turn radius and load × lift height. For multi-action windows where mast and traction overlap, integrate total pack power once.

Filter by capacity, clearance, stability, permitted lanes, traction, traffic reservations, deadline, and OEM current/temperature limits. For each surviving candidate predict net job Wh, duration, and a conservative **joint upper estimate** for job plus travel to an available charger, including expected queue draw. Admit it only if available energy above the reserve covers that estimate and the reserve remains feasible along the route. Use deliverable capacity at the observed state of health and temperature. A downhill segment can return energy subject to pack acceptance and headroom. For the first pilot, compare a finite set of feasible paths and propagate pack state in order. The RMF adapter can submit the selected itinerary for coordination, and Nav2 can execute it locally. Open-RMF provides separate motion and device drain interfaces and task-planner recharge parameters. [Open-RMF battery interfaces](https://github.com/open-rmf/rmf_battery/blob/main/README.md) · [Fleet adapter task-planner parameters](https://github.com/open-rmf/rmf_ros2/blob/main/rmf_fleet_adapter/include/rmf_fleet_adapter/agv/FleetUpdateHandle.hpp) · [Existing pre-dispatch selector](../scripts/warehouse-energy/route_selector.py)

Among eligible candidates, select the lowest expected Wh for the same unit of completed work, including required repositioning. For estimates that differ by less than the validated decision margin, prefer the earlier arrival or retain the current assignment. If every candidate fails the reserve check, schedule charging or defer the job and expose the limiting constraint to the operator.

**A check on the published telemetry.** I joined CMU's `parameters.csv` to `trials.zip` for route A, 1.0 m/s speed limit, and zero payload. On all three matching runs I integrated `-battery_voltage_husky × battery_current_husky` by trapezoids over the published elapsed seconds, using the ROS discharge convention documented in its README. The resulting signed integrals were **−7.87, +1.65, and −0.87 Wh** for trials 3, 21, and 31 respectively. All adjacent intervals were positive and under 0.5 s. These are reproducible readings from one channel and an assumed sign. Their mixed signs leave the interpretation as mission draw unresolved. A known-load polarity check, zero-offset measurement, and reconciliation against a reference meter are needed before using those runs as consumption labels. ## From a continuous power stream to dispatch data

| Signal to collect | Likely source | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timestamp, pack voltage (V), signed current (A) or signed real power (W), charger-connected state, cumulative energy counter if available | Read-only OEM BMS/CAN gateway, ROS driver, or calibrated bidirectional DC power logger at the pack. Clearpath documents `platform/bms/state`. ROS `BatteryState.current` uses negative for discharge and allows NaN for unmeasured current. | Confirm polarity during a known discharge and charge. Measure each independently powered subsystem needed for the chosen boundary. Reconcile integrated Wh with a reference meter over a full cycle. [Clearpath platform API](https://docs.clearpathrobotics.com/docs/ros/api/platform_api/) · [ROS message and units](https://github.com/ros2/common_interfaces/blob/rolling/sensor_msgs/msg/BatteryState.msg) |
| Charge fraction, pack ID, chemistry, usable capacity, temperature, available limits and health | OEM BMS, service records, charger logs, periodic capacity measurements. In VDA 5050 v2.1 the state schema includes charge and optional voltage. In v3.0 `powerSupply` adds optional `batteryCurrent`. | Normalize VDA's 0–100% and ROS's 0–1 fraction. Commission the actual device's fields, update rate and freshness. A rounded percentage feed can support dispatch status. Fine-grained Wh labels require metered power or a sufficiently resolved energy counter. [VDA 2.1 schema](https://github.com/VDA5050/VDA5050/blob/2.1.0/json_schemas/state.schema) · [VDA 3.0 schema](https://github.com/VDA5050/VDA5050/blob/3.0.0/json_schemas/state.schema) |
| Timestamped pose, directed edge, measured and commanded speed, yaw rate, stop duration, grade, wheel/track speed, surface | Odometry/encoders, IMU, map, localization, navigation action and RMF itinerary logs | Compare the path dispatched with the path actually driven. Annotate unexpected waits and detours. Use independent body motion to identify slip when encoder travel overstates progress. [Measured rover and maps](https://starslab.ca/enav-planetary-dataset/) |
| Pallet ID and weight, fork height/tilt, lifting/lowering command, time of transfer, attachment | Warehouse management system (WMS) order or scale, load cell or calibrated mast pressure, lift encoder and vehicle controller | Keep declared and measured weight with source, time and uncertainty. Treat an unknown weight as unknown. Split an otherwise identical drive into empty and loaded legs at the actual handoff. [Linde sensor implementation](https://www.linde-mh.com/en/About-us/Innovations-from-Linde/Linde-Safety-Pilot.html) |

1. **Capture at the device.** Prefer simultaneous voltage/current or directly metered power. An initial test rate of 10 Hz is grounded in CMU's mobile dataset. Compare the result with a faster reference around short accelerations and mast transients, and increase the acquisition rate when energy error or peak aliasing warrants it. If the available BMS publishes only once per second, keep its native timestamps. Use a logger or cumulative meter for the missing detail. Continuous sampling tracks *varying* power over time. [CMU acquisition method](https://doi.org/10.1184/R1/13623944.v1) · [Forklift CAN measurement](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/18/9854)
2. **Integrate at the battery boundary.** Normalize to discharge-positive power, so for a conforming ROS feed `P_out = -V × I`. For each valid adjacent pair accumulate `0.5 × (P_out[i] + P_out[i+1]) × Δt_seconds / 3600` Wh. Keep outbound Wh, returned Wh and their net separately, splitting intervals at zero crossings. Tag dock charging separately from onboard recovery. As a unit check, 48 V at 10 A discharge for 90 s delivers 12 Wh. Deduplicate by device, boot/session and sequence. Flag clock resets, gaps, stale pairs, clipping, missing channels, and coverage loss. Keep partial windows identifiable and exclude them from calibration unless an independent counter reconciles them.
3. **Attribute to the job.** Synchronize device clocks, preserve acquisition and receipt times, and join by robot, pack, mission, action and map version. Attach time windows for dispatch, empty approach, pickup, loaded travel, lift/lower, traffic wait, return and charging. Use mutually exclusive accounting windows for totals and concurrent activity flags for explanation. Reconcile segment totals to mission Wh. Benchmark completed missions with the same accounting window, and include aborted work in shift totals.
4. **Move data off the robot at the right resolution.** Buffer raw timestamped frames locally in a rotating ROS bag/MCAP or gateway log. Publish sequence-numbered batches over the customer's existing MQTT/Kafka or equivalent gateway and replay after outages. Store immutable raw traces and compact per-segment training rows in S3-compatible storage. Give the fleet manager a low-rate current status and last valid reserve estimate. The ingestion gateway, clock alignment and telemetry schema are integration work. OpenShift AI can consume the resulting dataset through a data connection. [Red Hat OpenShift AI data connections](https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_openshift_ai_self-managed/3.5/html/working_on_projects/using-connections_projects)
5. **Prevent leakage.** Archive the exact candidates, payload information and planned speeds available at dispatch, plus the chosen robot, policy and model version. Actual slip, actual duration, measured current and final pallet weight belong on the outcome side unless known at decision time. A model trained only on previously selected corridors cannot establish what the unchosen one would have consumed. Collect matched traversals of both routes within the approved operating envelope.

## How to decide whether to roll it out

1. **Meter and design the comparison.** With the customer, fix a meaningful Wh/job threshold, acceptable service time, reserve policy, and reference-meter tolerance. Run matched tasks in randomized order across routes, load bands and directions. Block by vehicle, shift, starting charge and temperature. Measure idle power first. Use pilot variance and meter uncertainty to size the paired trial. A measurable change in surface resistance or stops offers a better experiment than two ramps with the same vertical rise and otherwise similar losses.
2. **Qualify the forecast and the decision separately.** Hold out complete missions, days and physical units. Report separately on an unseen surface or hardware configuration. Compare distance/nearest-capable dispatch, calibrated physics, and physics plus the learned correction under identical feasibility rules. Evaluate Wh bias/MAE, mission upper-bound coverage, and observed ranking regret (excess Wh over the best measured feasible alternative). Use repeated matched runs to estimate that alternative. Calibrate uncertainty on complete missions so correlated segment errors survive aggregation.
3. **Qualify operations.** Run in shadow mode and then compare matched operating periods. Report completed and refused jobs, deadline misses, low-reserve incidents, fallback frequency, and serving latency alongside Wh/job. Report a paired confidence interval against *both* baselines, and include the cost of metering and serving in the business case. For utility savings, multiply the measured change in **wall kWh per equivalent job** by annual volume and the customer's tariff. Evaluate peak-demand charges separately. Pack Wh and wall kWh answer different questions.
4. **Substantiate battery preservation at the relevant timescale.** For one shift, measure minimum reserve, high-current exposure, temperature, depth of discharge and charge throughput. For a service-life claim, follow capacity or resistance at comparable conditions over months, with pack chemistry, age, charge policy and throughput recorded. Compare with matched packs or a chemistry-specific aging model validated against the fleet. A lower-energy mission and a delayed battery replacement require different evidence. [National Laboratory of the Rockies battery lifetime models](https://www.nlr.gov/transportation/blast)

**Rollout decision.** If lower forecast error enables more jobs to clear the reserve check within the customer's limits, report that dispatch benefit. If the learned planner also reduces metered Wh per equivalent job beyond the agreed margin while meeting throughput and reserve requirements, report that energy benefit separately. If physics makes the same decisions at the same cost, keep it in dispatch and use the platform for monitoring, recalibration and forecasting where those functions earn their operating cost.
