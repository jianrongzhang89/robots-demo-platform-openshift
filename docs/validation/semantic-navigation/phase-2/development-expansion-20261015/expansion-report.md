## Candidate set

Captured stopped 640×480 RGB-D views in simulator episodes 3–6, varying viewpoint yaw and target range. The capture wrapper records direct per-capture Gazebo `world_from_base_link`, RGB-D, CameraInfo, static TF, odometry, and timestamps. Positive candidate masks use projected target geometry checked against depth; negative masks are empty candidates tied to intended target-absent conditions.

The capture pool contained 50 image files. SHA-256 comparison found one exact duplicate: the canonical initial views from episodes 5 and 6. That duplicate is excluded. The curated review set uses 12 new positive and 19 new negative candidates, alongside the nine previously reviewed pilot samples, yielding 40 unique rows: 20 positive and 20 negative across six episode IDs. The unused captures remain available as diagnostics but are not in the review manifest.

## Data-quality findings

- Visual inspection caught four episode-3 views originally intended as tile negatives that still showed part of the red mat at the frame edge. They were reclassified as positive candidates using new versioned depth/geometry masks; the original empty masks remain in place for audit and are not selected in the review manifest.
- The first episode-5 and episode-6 canonical positive captures were exact byte duplicates. Only the episode-5 frame is selected.
- Several capture attempts were rejected: RGB/depth timestamps separated by roughly 33–35 seconds during camera stalls, and the Gazebo one-shot pose command intermittently exited with SIGSEGV/SIGABRT. No such attempts appear in the curated manifest. Successful frames have synchronized metadata and passed the dataset validator.
- The capture wrapper now retries a transient Gazebo pose-query failure up to three times, rejects non-zero command exits, malformed/missing robot poses, non-finite coordinates, and non-unit quaternions, and reports the attempt errors instead of forwarding a partial pose to the camera capture CLI. The parser passed valid/malformed/non-unit test cases. A live read-only query against the running simulator returned robot pose `(15.4014, -31.5940, 0.0100)` with normalized quaternion; the camera compressor was producing paired frames with 0 ms RGB-depth skew at the time of inspection. This confirms current health, not the root cause of historical camera stalls.
- Follow-up read-only stream diagnostics on 2026-10-15: the receiver-side compressed-stream probe had run for about 520 wall seconds and received 1,430 RGB and 1,430 depth messages, of which 1,428 pairs met the synchronization/layout checks; it reported zero invalid frames and zero missing TF lookups. A separate short `ros2 topic hz` sample on the raw topics was much less regular (observed maximum inter-arrival gaps of 2.177 s for color and 1.557 s for depth). At one `oc adm top pod` snapshot, the simulator used about 8.3 CPU cores against a 12-core container limit and 2.1 GiB against an 8 GiB memory limit. This is evidence of low/variable simulator throughput under load, not proof of CPU throttling or the cause of the earlier 33–35 s RGB-depth timestamp divergence. The existing capture code rejects pairs above its 30 ms RGB-depth skew gate; no bad pair entered the curated manifest.
- A later 100-second camera-compressor log interval narrows the current throughput issue: raw callback counters advanced by 809 color and 847 depth messages (~8.1/~8.5 Hz), while synchronized compressor output advanced by 301 pairs (~3.0 Hz). During this interval the queue reached its 10-RGB capacity and one reported nearest-frame gap was 500 ms; other status reports showed 0 ms gaps. A separate 40-message Gazebo `/world/sim_world/stats` sample spanned 4.674 s wall time and 3.72 s simulation time (aggregate real-time factor ~0.796; instantaneous field varied 0.038–1.297 and is noisy). The evidence indicates current frame-pair loss associated with timing skew and/or variable simulation/render cadence. It does not prove which mechanism dominates or establish that this caused the earlier 33–35 s mismatch. Do not widen the 30 ms synchronization limit without a demonstrated capture-alignment test.
- Repeated the raw-topic check with reliable QoS to avoid the best-effort sample's possible undercount. Over 20 wall seconds, it received 153 color and 153 depth messages at 8.84/8.74 Hz by source stamps. Callback delivery was bursty (wall-time gap p95 0.492/0.535 s, maxima 1.10/0.86 s). A greedy one-to-one timestamp match paired 136/153 RGB frames within 30 ms, all at exactly 0 ms skew. Replaying that same ordered callback stream through the old compressor policy produced 53 pairs, 121 skew rejections, 13/12 queue evictions, and 197 extra buffered frames discarded on queue clear. A timestamp-ordered FIFO simulation produced 151 pairs, discarded 28 unmatched frames, and had zero queue evictions. This isolates the measured rate loss to the old matching policy under burst delivery; it does not explain the historical 33–35 s outlier.
- Implemented the timestamp-ordered one-to-one queue matcher locally in `camera_compressor.py` via the pure helper `rgbd_pairing.py`. The existing 30 ms tolerance is unchanged. Unit tests cover batched aligned frames, tolerance-boundary behavior, unmatched buffering, and invalid tolerance. The helper was first tested as a read-only subscriber against live raw topics: a 20 s pairing-only sample formed 151 pairs (7.55/s); a second 20 s sample also ran the RGB/JPEG and depth/PNG encoders without publishing and encoded 122 pairs (6.1/s), with zero encoding failures and encode latency p50/p95/max 6.34/30.95/95.39 ms.

- The final compressor counters are a separate efficiency caveat: over its slightly longer startup-to-end interval it reported 5,413 pairs from 5,823 RGB and 5,778 depth callbacks, 773 timestamp discards, maximum observed skew 500 ms, queue high-water marks 9/10, and zero queue evictions. - To determine whether this loss is caused by premature greedy matching, a separate 120-second read-only trace captured raw callbacks on the still-running canonical simulator and replayed the same callback sequence through 10- and 100-message FIFO queues, then computed an offline maximum one-to-one timestamp match. It received 1,029 RGB and 1,045 depth messages, with no source-stamp inversions. The 10-slot online replay made 1,006 pairs and evicted 9 RGB/6 depth queue entries; the 100-slot replay made 1,008 pairs with zero evictions, equal to the offline maximum. All 1,008 offline pairs had exactly 0 ms source-stamp skew; 21 RGB and 37 depth messages had no counterpart within 30 ms. For this canonical-stream sample, enlarging the queue recovered only two more pairs, so most unmatched frames were not recoverable by waiting for callbacks under the configured timestamp gate. Do not widen the gate: the adjacent unmatched frames need not depict the same instant. The 10-minute diagnostic scene's higher 6.7% unmatched-input figure was not independently replayed offline, so its precise avoidable-versus-source-unmatched split remains unmeasured.
- Extended that same read-only raw-topic replay to 600.057 s on the canonical simulator. It received 5,734 RGB and 5,794 depth callbacks with no source-stamp inversions. A timestamp-ordered offline maximum paired 5,729; all matched pairs had exactly 0 ms skew, leaving 5 RGB and 65 depth messages with no counterpart within 30 ms. The online 10-slot matcher made 5,726 pairs (one RGB and 31 depth queue evictions); replay with 100 slots made 5,729, with no evictions, equal to the offline maximum. Thus queue growth recovered just three pairs (~0.05% of offline pairs) over this longer sample, and the residual unpaired share was about 0.61% of total raw callbacks. The longer live trace supports the earlier conclusion that queue capacity is a minor contributor under canonical-scene conditions; it does not explain the separate isolated-soak result (6.7% unmatched) because that run's raw callback sequence was not captured for offline replay. Keep the 30 ms gate unchanged; widening it is not justified by these measurements.

- Investigated the yield loss against the deployed raw topics without publishing or changing the Deployment. - A same-process QoS comparison isolated a major upstream loss mechanism. The compressor subscribes with `QoSProfile(depth=2, reliability=RELIABLE)`. With paired depth-2 and depth-20 reliable observers on the same raw topics for 120 seconds, depth 2 received 1,107 RGB/1,093 depth versus 1,167/1,170 at depth 20 (5.1%/6.6% fewer). The large history recovers most of the missing pairability, showing that shallow DDS subscriber history under bursty delivery is a major contributor to the observed loss. The depth-2 test did not perform image encoding, so synchronous encoding/backpressure may contribute additional loss; that residual is not yet isolated. The production Deployment was left unchanged.
